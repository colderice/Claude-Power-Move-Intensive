There are two ways to use Claude. Most people are stuck on the wrong one.

The first way is to treat Claude like Google. You show up with a question. You get an answer. You close the tab. Maybe you copy something. Maybe you don't.

That's not nothing. But it's not a business advantage either.

The second way is to build Claude into your operation. You stop asking it questions and start giving it jobs. Recurring jobs. Jobs that used to eat your week.

The shift that changes everything

Stop asking Claude what to write. Start telling Claude what your business needs and letting it run the process. That's when Claude goes from interesting to indispensable.

Here's a practical example. Instead of typing "write me a follow-up email," try this:

Try this prompt instead

"You are my follow-up system. Every time I give you a prospect name and where the conversation left off, you write me a 3-touch follow-up sequence in my voice. Keep it short, direct, and focused on next steps. Here's the first one: [prospect details]."

That's not a one-time ask. That's a repeatable system. You run it every week. Claude does the same job every time. You stop starting from scratch.

The tasks worth systemizing first

01   Follow-up sequences for leads and open proposals
02   Weekly content — posts, emails, and captions from one input
03   Client onboarding docs, SOPs, and intake summaries
04   Proposal and offer copy that sounds like you, not a template

Pick one. Build the prompt around it. Run it the same way every week. That's how you stop being a Claude user and start being a Claude operator.

Operators win. Users stay busy.
