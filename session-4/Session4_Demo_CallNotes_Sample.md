## THE OFFER (What Was Proposed)

**Service:** 6-Month Business Growth Coaching Program
**Investment:** $4,800 (paid in full) or $850/month for 6 months
**Scope:**
- Weekly 60-minute 1:1 coaching calls
- Unlimited voice memo / Voxer access between sessions
- Custom 90-day action plan built in month one
- Access to client portal with templates, training, and resources
- Quarterly business review and pivot session

**Proposal sent:** Today, via email
**Subject line used:** "Your 6-Month Growth Plan — [Client Name]"

---

## THE CALL NOTES

**Client:** David Rivera
**Business:** Rivera Landscaping & Grounds Management
**Location:** Charlotte, NC
**Team:** 8 employees (6 field, 1 office manager, 1 part-time estimator)
**Current revenue:** Approx. $620K/year
**Goal:** Hit $1M within 18 months

---

### What He Told Me on the Call

**The core problem:**
David is the business. Everything runs through him — every estimate, every client complaint, every hiring decision. He said: *"If I take a vacation, the whole thing wobbles."* He knows he needs to build systems and a real management layer but doesn't know where to start. He's tried hiring a foreman twice — both left within 90 days.

**His biggest frustration:**
He's doing $620K but working like he's doing $200K. Long days, no real days off, and he said his wife has mentioned more than once that the business owns him instead of the other way around.

**Revenue inconsistency:**
Heavy in spring/summer, dead in November–February. He hasn't cracked how to smooth out the off-season revenue. Lost two commercial accounts last year that would have helped — didn't follow up fast enough on the renewal.

**What he's tried:**
- Hired a business coach 2 years ago — said it was "too general, felt like I was paying for someone to tell me what I already knew"
- Tried implementing a CRM last year, gave up after 6 weeks
- Has read the E-Myth, Buy Back Your Time — knows the theory, struggling with the execution in his specific business

**What got him on the call:**
Saw a post about systems and AI making small businesses run without the owner being the bottleneck. Said it spoke directly to his situation.

**Positive signals:**
- Asked detailed questions about what months 2–4 look like — that's a buyer thinking forward
- Asked if other trades businesses had gone through the program
- Said: *"I don't need motivation, I need a blueprint. Can you actually give me that?"*
- Stayed on 15 minutes past the scheduled end of the call

**His hesitation:**
Price. Not the dollar amount per se — he said *"I've wasted money on coaches before"* and wanted to know what makes this different. Also mentioned timing: Q2 is his busiest season and he's worried about bandwidth.

**How the call ended:**
Warm. Said he'd review the proposal over the weekend and get back to me Monday. Didn't say no. Didn't say yes. Engaged throughout.

**Temperature:** Warm — high interest, real hesitation about past coaching disappointment and seasonal timing.

## WHAT TO POINT OUT IN THE OUTPUT

When Claude delivers the three touches, call out these specific references for the audience:

- **Touch 1** should feel personal — not a generic "just following up." Watch for it to reference David by name and the specific conversation.
- **Touch 2** should reference one of his specific pain points — the bottleneck problem, the winter revenue drop, or the past coaching disappointment.
- **Touch 3** should acknowledge the weekend timeline he gave and the Q2 timing concern — not just a generic close.

**The teaching line:**
*"Claude read the notes. I didn't write any of that — I gave it the context and it pulled what mattered. That message knows David. A generic follow-up doesn't close David. This one might."*
