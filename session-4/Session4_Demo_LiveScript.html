Projects Overview

What Is a Claude Project?

Show this on screen — navigate to Claude Projects in the sidebar before you explain. Let them see the UI as you talk.

Regular Chat vs. Project

When you open Claude and start a regular chat, it knows nothing about you. Every conversation starts cold. You have to explain who you are, what you do, how you want it to sound — every single time.

A Project is different. A Project has a permanent system prompt — a set of instructions that is always loaded, always active, every time you open it. You set it once. It never forgets.

Where to Find It

On screen: show the left sidebar in Claude. Point to "Projects" and click to open one.
On Screen — Show This

Left sidebar → Projects. Each Project is a separate workspace. Click into one and you see the conversation history plus the system prompt in the top-right panel. That system prompt is the SOP — it loads automatically every time someone starts a conversation inside that Project.

Four Things That Make a Project Different

One — the system prompt is always on. You don't paste it. You don't reference it. It just runs in the background every time.

Two — it remembers the conversation history. Every session inside the Project adds to the context. Your departments learn your patterns over time.

Three — you can upload files. Add your proposal template. Add your brand guide. Add a past client summary. The department has access to whatever you put in there.

Four — it's yours. Nobody else's. Nobody else's prompts, nobody else's training data influencing it. This is your department, configured for your business.

Session 2 Built This. Session 4 Runs on It.

The Concept

In Session 2, we built something important. We gave Claude your voice — who you are, who you serve, how you talk, what you never say. That became your voice profile.

Tonight that voice profile becomes the foundation of every department we build. Not a separate thing you paste in each time. The DNA that lives inside every Project so every department already sounds like you before you type a single word.

How to Build a Department System Prompt

Here's the exact workflow. Three steps.

One — open a fresh Claude chat. Not a Project. Just a regular chat.

Two — paste in your voice profile. Then paste in the department SOP — the job description, the output format, the rules.

Three — ask Claude to compress both into a single system prompt. One tight block of instructions you can paste directly into a Project.

If the result is still too long, you prompt: "Cut this down by 30%. Keep all the voice rules and the full SOP. Remove anything redundant."

Claude writes its own instructions. You review. You paste it into the Project. Done.

Segment 4 — 12 Min 📞 Follow-Up Department

Follow-Up Department

Beat 1 — The Flip

Department two is where most businesses are losing real money — and this one changes immediately.

Most follow-up is reactive. Lead goes cold, then you scramble to figure out what to say. This department flips that completely.

The rule is: the follow-up gets built the moment the proposal goes out.

You just got off the call. You send the proposal. Before you close the laptop — you open this department, paste in the proposal, and it writes your 3-touch follow-up sequence right then. Day 2, Day 5, Day 10. All written. All specific. Ready to send on schedule.

You never chase a cold lead again — because you never gave it time to get cold.

Beat 2 — The Transcript Reveal

And here's what makes it even better.

If you recorded the call — if you have a transcript from Zoom or Fathom or wherever — you paste that in too.

Now the follow-up messages reference specific things from the actual conversation. What they said they were struggling with. What they told you their goal was. What made them hesitate.

Claude read the call. The follow-up sounds like you were paying perfect attention — because Claude was.
Switch to the Follow-Up Department Project. Show the SOP loaded in the system prompt. Scroll through it briefly before running the demo.
Follow-Up Department — System Prompt (Pre-Loaded in Project) 
You are the Follow-Up Department for [Business Name].

Your job: Turn a proposal and/or call context into a ready-to-send follow-up sequence.

Every time you receive input, produce:
- Touch 1 (send Day 2): Warm check-in. Short. One clear ask.
- Touch 2 (send Day 5): Add one specific value point pulled directly from the proposal or call context. Soft CTA.
- Touch 3 (send Day 10): Honest close. Give them an easy out. Keep the door open.

Rules:
- Pull specific details from the proposal and transcript. Never write generic messages.
- Match the temperature — read how interested they seemed and write accordingly.
- Under 100 words per message.
- No "just checking in." No "I hope this finds you well."
- Write in [Name]'s voice: direct, warm, no corporate softness.

When you receive input, ask nothing. Run the SOP. Deliver all three touches.

Operations Department — System Prompt (Pre-Loaded in Project) 
You are the Operations Department for [Business Name].

You handle research, preparation, decision support, and operational tasks.

You know the business: [brief company description, offers, target client, current priorities]

When given a task or situation:
- Client call prep: produce 3 discovery questions, 2 situation questions, 1 key insight to share.
- Research request: the 3 things that matter most — not an exhaustive list.
- Document summary: key decisions, action items, anything needing follow-up.
- Anything else: ask one clarifying question, then execute.

Always be concise. Decision-ready output, not academic output.

Q&A — Common Questions

What goes in the system prompt?
Detailed enough that you wouldn't have to explain it again — like onboarding a smart new hire. We build these from scratch at the intensive, for your actual business.
Does this work on free Claude?
Projects require Claude Pro. Worth every penny — this is your operating system, not a tool you use once a week.
What if my call wasn't recorded?
Paste your notes instead of a transcript. Even rough notes give Claude enough to personalize the sequence. The key is giving it the context — however you captured it.
Can I have more than three departments?
Absolutely. These three are the highest-impact starting point. Once they're running, you'll know exactly what to add next. Build in order of where your time and money are going.
