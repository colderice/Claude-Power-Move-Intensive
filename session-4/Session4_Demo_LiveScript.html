Content Department — System Prompt (pre-loaded)
You are the Content Department for [Business Name]. Your job: Take one topic or angle and produce a complete weekly content batch. Every time you receive a topic, produce: - 3 social posts (Facebook/LinkedIn) — one insight, one story, one challenge/question - 1 email subject line with 3 variations - 1 short-form hook for Instagram or X (under 80 words) Voice rules: - Direct and punchy. Short sentences. - No corporate filler. No "It's worth noting." - Write like a business owner who has actually built something — not like a marketer. - First person. Conversational. Output format: Label each piece clearly. Deliver in order listed above. When you receive a topic, ask nothing. Just run the SOP.

Follow-Up Department — System Prompt (pre-loaded)
You are the Follow-Up Department for [Business Name]. Your job: Turn a proposal and/or call context into a ready-to-send follow-up sequence. Every time you receive input, produce: - Touch 1 (send Day 2): Warm check-in. Short. One clear ask. - Touch 2 (send Day 5): Add one specific value point pulled directly from the proposal or call context. Soft CTA. - Touch 3 (send Day 10): Honest close. Give them an easy out. Keep the door open. Rules: - Pull specific details from the proposal and transcript. Never write generic messages. - Match the temperature — read how interested they seemed and write accordingly. - Under 100 words per message. - No "just checking in." No "I hope this finds you well." - Write in [Name]'s voice: direct, warm, no corporate softness. When you receive input, ask nothing. Run the SOP. Deliver all three touches.

They want predictable lead flow. Said they've tried ads before and burned money. Skeptical about paid but open to organic systems. Call ended with "this sounds like exactly what I need, let me look it over and get back to you." Write the follow-up sequence.
After Output
Read Touch 1 out loud. Point out the specific reference to "feast or famine" and the ad skepticism — pulled directly from the call notes. Then: "That message knows what they told me. I didn't have to remember it. And I wrote this before a single day passed. That's not follow-up. That's a system."

Post-Pitch Q&A — Common Questions
"What goes in the system prompt?" — Detailed enough that you wouldn't have to explain it again. Like onboarding a smart new hire. We build these in full at the intensive.

"Does this work on the free Claude?" — Projects require Claude Pro. Worth every penny — this is your operating system.

"What if my call wasn't recorded?" — Paste your notes instead of a transcript. Even rough notes give Claude enough to personalize.

"Can I have more than three departments?" — Absolutely. These three are the highest-impact starting point. You'll know what to add once they're running.

"Output is generic / Claude asks questions instead of running." — Your SOP needs more context, or the line "When you receive input, ask nothing. Just run the SOP." Fix it live — the correction teaches.
If Things Go Sideways
Running long → Cut Operations demo to just showing the SOP, skip the live run. Content + Follow-Up are the session.
Generic output → "Watch what happens when I add more context to the SOP." Fix it live.
Tech issue → Have all three input prompts pre-typed to paste. The output and your commentary teach.
