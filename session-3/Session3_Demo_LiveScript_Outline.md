## SECTION 6 — THE 3-PROMPT STACK

---

### PROMPT 1 — The Context Brief (Strategy First)

**What you're doing:** Before asking Claude to write anything, you're giving it the full picture — including the original proposal email — and asking for its recommended approach. This models the right behavior: directing, not just prompting.

**The prompt covers:**
- Who you are (marketing consultant)
- What happened (discovery call, $3,500 proposal, 3 days of silence)
- Paste or reference the original proposal email so Claude has the full context of what was sent
- Lead temperature (warm — interested, just quiet)
- The ask: recommend the timing, tone, and angle for each of 3 touches — do not write them yet

**What to watch for:** A strategic approach, not a generic "follow up!" suggestion. It should differentiate touch 1 (light check-in), touch 2 (value add), touch 3 (honest close).

**What to say after:** You're approving the approach before you approve the writing. Most people skip this step and wonder why their follow-up sounds like a template. Claude just read the same email your lead read — now it knows what it's working with.

---

### PROMPT 2 — Write the 3-Touch Sequence

**What you're doing:** Claude has the context, the original email, and the approved strategy. Now it executes.

**The prompt covers:**
- Message 1 (today): Light check-in. Friendly. Not pushy. One easy ask.
- Message 2 (3 days): More direct. One value piece — an insight relevant to their business. Soft CTA to jump on a quick call.
- Message 3 (7 days): The honest close. Give them the easy out. Door stays open.
- Under 100 words each. No "just checking in." No "I hope this finds you well." Sounds like you wrote it at the right moment, not like a drip sequence.

**What to watch for:** Three distinct messages that escalate appropriately. Each one tight. No generic opener.

**What to say after:** Read Message 1 out loud. React naturally — what's strong, what you'd tweak. Show the editing decision. "This is the $200-an-hour work. Not writing. Deciding."

---

### PROMPT 3 — Adapt for a Different Scenario

**What you're doing:** Show the framework transfers. Same inputs, different temperature — one more minute of work, completely different use case.

**The scenario:** Same service, same consultant. But this is a past client — ended on good terms, haven't worked together in 8 months. No previous email to reference here — the history is the context. You want to reopen the conversation without it feeling like a sales call.

**The prompt covers:**
- Same service
- Different temperature — warmer. They know you. No pitch energy.
- One message. Personal. Opens the door without pushing through it.

**What to say after:** Same framework. Different temperature. Different message. Didn't start over — adapted. One proposal follow-up and one past client re-engagement, out of one conversation. That's the system.
