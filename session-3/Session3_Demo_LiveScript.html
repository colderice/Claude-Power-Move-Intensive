Philosophy

The Shift Behind This Session

Most business owners aren't losing money because they can't close. They're losing it because they stop showing up after the first message.

The problem isn't the lead. It's the silence after the proposal. And the silence happens because there's no system — every follow-up is a brand-new creative task that competes with everything else in the day. So it just doesn't happen.

Claude doesn't get awkward on the third follow-up. It doesn't forget. It doesn't feel like it's bothering someone. It executes on command, every time, in your voice.

The shift you're demonstrating: from hoping they'll respond — to having a system that follows up whether you feel like it or not.

Beat 2 — The Three Real Reasons

Here's the thing. It wasn't because you didn't care. It wasn't because the lead was bad. It comes down to three reasons — and I've seen them pull money out of business owners' pockets over and over.

One: you forgot. There was no system to remind you, so it fell off the list.

Three: every follow-up was a brand-new creative task. You had to decide what to say, how to say it, when to send it — from scratch — every single time. That's exhausting. So you just didn't do it.

A strategic breakdown — not a list of generic tips. Touch 1 should be a light, friendly check-in. Touch 2 should add a value piece. Touch 3 should be an honest close with an easy out. If it's vague, push back: "Be more specific about the angle for each touch."

Prompt 2 of 3

Write the 3-Touch Sequence

Claude has the full context. It's seen the original email Marcus received. We've agreed on the strategy. Now we build the actual messages.
Prompt to Paste Live 
Good. That approach works. Now write all three messages.

Message 1 (today): Light check-in. Friendly. Not pushy. Make it easy to respond. One clear ask.

Message 2 (3 days from now): A little more direct. Include one specific observation or insight relevant to his business — something that shows I was paying attention on our call. Soft CTA to jump on a quick 15-minute call.

Message 3 (7 days from now): The honest close. Acknowledge the silence directly. Give him an easy out — if the timing is off or it's not the right fit, say so. Keep the door open.

Rules for all three:
- Under 100 words each
- No "just checking in"
- No "I hope this finds you well"
- No em dashes
- Sounds like I wrote it personally at the right moment — not like a drip sequence
What You Should See

Three distinct messages that each have a different job. Message 1 is light. Message 2 earns the reply with value. Message 3 is honest and confident. None of them should open with a generic line. If one does, fix it live — that's the directing lesson.
