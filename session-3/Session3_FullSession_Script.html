Segment 2

Why Follow-Up Fails 5 min

There are three reasons people don't follow up. And I want to name them because once you hear them, you're going to recognize yourself in at least one of them.

One — they forget. Life is busy and follow-up gets pushed every single time something urgent shows up. Which is always. The lead goes on a mental list. The list never gets worked.

Two — they don't know what to say. The first message felt natural — you had context, you had momentum. The second one feels awkward. By the third, you're convinced you're annoying them. So you stop.

Three — there's no system. Every follow-up is a brand new creative task. You have to decide what to say, when to send it, what tone to hit — every single time. That's exhausting. So you just don't do it.

Here's what Claude does. It removes all three problems at once.

You tell Claude who you're following up with, where the last conversation left off, and what you want them to do next. Claude writes the sequence. You review it. You send it.

No blank screen. No "I don't know what to say." No leads going cold because you ran out of bandwidth.

Let me show you exactly how it works.

Segment 3

The Follow-Up Framework 10 min

Teaching segment — no screen share yet. Use the framework grid below as your mental map. This is the foundation everything else builds on.

Before we get into Claude, I want to give you the framework we're building from tonight. Because Claude is the tool — but you need to know how to use it. And the framework is simple.

Every follow-up situation has three variables. Get these right and Claude can write something that sounds like you sent it at exactly the right moment. Miss any one of them and you're going to get a generic message that sounds like every other follow-up in your inbox.

01
Temperature
Is this a warm lead, a cold prospect, or a past client? The message has to match where they are — not where you want them to be. Warm is different from cold. Past client is different from both.
02
Context
What happened in the last conversation? Proposal sent? Discovery call done? They said "send me more info" and went quiet? Claude needs the actual context to write something that feels real.
03
The Ask
What do you want them to do? Reply? Book a call? Review the proposal? One clear next step per message. Never two. One ask that's easy to say yes to.

Temperature. Context. Ask. That's the input. Claude handles the output.

When you give Claude those three things, it can write follow-up that sounds like you wrote it personally — not like you set up a drip campaign and hoped for the best.

Write the 3-Touch Sequence

Claude has the full context. It's read the original email Marcus received. We've agreed on the strategy. Now we execute.

Prompt to Paste Live 
Good. That approach works. Now write all three messages.

Message 1 (today): Light check-in. Friendly. Not pushy. Make it easy to respond. One clear ask.

Message 2 (3 days from now): A little more direct. Include one specific observation or insight relevant to his business — something that shows I was paying attention on our call. Soft CTA to jump on a quick 15-minute call.

Message 3 (7 days from now): The honest close. Acknowledge the silence directly. Give him an easy out — if the timing is off or it's not the right fit, say so. Keep the door open.

Rules for all three:
- Under 100 words each
- No "just checking in"
- No "I hope this finds you well"
- No em dashes
- Sounds like I wrote it personally at the right moment — not like a drip sequence

Three distinct messages, each with a different job. Message 1 is easy to respond to. Message 2 earns the reply with something useful. Message 3 is confident and honest — no desperation. None of them open with "I."

Segment 7

Q&A — Common Questions 5–10 min

These are the questions that come up almost every session. Know the answers cold so you're not hunting for words when someone asks.
"What if I use a CRM — can Claude work with that?"
Claude writes the copy. Your CRM sends it. They're separate tools — Claude is your writing partner, your CRM is your delivery system. You take what Claude produces, drop it into your CRM sequence, and let it run. They work together — they just have different jobs.
"What if I don't know what temperature my lead is?"
Tell Claude what you do know. The details of the call, what they said, how they responded, what they seemed excited about. Claude will help you read the situation and recommend the right approach. You don't need a perfect label — you need the context. Claude figures out the rest.
"How do I know it won't sound like AI?"
It sounds like you because your voice profile is loaded in the Project. That's the whole point of the Session 1 setup — you build the profile once, and from that point on Claude is writing in your voice, not in some generic AI voice. If you haven't built your voice profile yet, go to john.tips/voice-builder. It takes about ten minutes. Then run tonight's prompts and see the difference.
"Can I use this for cold outreach — not just warm leads?"
Yes — same framework, different temperature input. Cold requires more context up front: who they are, why you're reaching out, what you know about their business or situation. The colder the lead, the more context Claude needs to write something that doesn't feel like a blast. But the framework is the same. We can go deeper on cold outreach in a future session.
