## SEGMENT 3 — THE FOLLOW-UP FRAMEWORK (10 min)

### The 3-Touch Model

"Before we get into Claude, I want to give you the framework we're going to build from.

Every follow-up situation has three key variables:

**The temperature.** Is this a warm lead, a cold prospect, or a past client? The message needs to match where they are — not where you want them to be.

**The context.** What happened in the last conversation? Proposal sent? Discovery call completed? They expressed interest and went quiet? Claude needs that context to write something that feels real, not generic.

**The ask.** What do you want them to do? Reply? Book a call? Review a proposal? One clear next step per message. Never two.

When you give Claude those three things — temperature, context, ask — it can write follow-up that sounds like you wrote it at the right moment, not like a drip campaign you set up in a hurry.

Tonight's demo: a service business sent a proposal three days ago. No response. I'm going to build a complete 3-touch sequence live — what to send now, what to send in three days, and what to send in a week if there's still no response."

### PROMPT 1 — CONTEXT BRIEF

**Type this live:**
```
I need help writing a follow-up sequence.

Context: I'm a marketing consultant. I had a great discovery call with a small business owner last week. Sent a $3,500 proposal for a 90-day marketing strategy engagement three days ago. No response. Help me write a 3-touch follow-up sequence over the next 7 days. Each message should:
- Sound like I wrote it personally, not like an automated drip
- Match the temperature — warm but not desperate
- Have one clear ask per message
- Be short — under 100 words each

Don't write them yet. First tell me your recommended approach for each touch: timing, tone, and angle.
```

**What to say after:**

### PROMPT 2 — WRITE THE SEQUENCE

**Type this live:**
```
Good. Now write all three messages.

Message 1 (today): Light check-in. Friendly. Not pushy. Remind them I'm here and make it easy to respond.

Message 2 (3 days from now): A little more direct. Add a small value piece — one insight or observation relevant to their business. Soft CTA to get on a quick call.

Message 3 (7 days from now): The honest close. If this isn't a fit or the timing is off, say so. Give them an easy out — but make clear the door stays open.

Match my voice. No corporate softness. Short sentences. No "I hope this finds you well." No "just checking in."
```

**What to say after:**

## Q&A NOTES

**Common questions to expect:**

*"What if I use a CRM — can Claude work with that?"*
Claude writes the copy. Your CRM sends it. They're separate tools — Claude is your writing partner, your CRM is your delivery system.

*"What if I don't know what temperature my lead is?"*
Tell Claude what you do know. The call details, what they said, how they responded. Claude will help you read it and pick the right approach.

*"How do I know it won't sound like AI?"*
It sounds like you because your voice profile is loaded. That's the whole point of Session 1 setup. If you haven't built your voice profile yet — john.tips/voice-builder. Ten minutes. Then try tonight's prompts yourself.

*"Can I do this for cold outreach, not just warm leads?"*
Yes — same framework, different temperature. Cold requires more context about who they are and why you're reaching out. We can go deeper on that in a future session.
