### Teach the Voice Profile (what it is, what goes in it)

"So let me show you what's actually in a Voice Profile, because some of you used the tool and you might not even know what you built. Let me walk you through it."

*(Pull up your own Voice Profile on screen — same one from your Claude Project.)*

"Seven sections. And I'm going to walk through all seven real quick.

First — who you are. Not your resume, not your LinkedIn. The version that tells Claude what makes you credible and what kind of work you actually do. For me it's something like: AI strategy consultant, keynote speaker, 20 years in the trenches, $32M in career sales. Claude knows that. Every time I open that project, it knows that.

Second — your audience. And here's where most people are too vague. You can't say 'small business owners.' Claude needs to know what they're afraid of, what they're tired of hearing, what they actually want. The more specific you are here, the better everything that comes out sounds.

Third — your offers. What you're selling. What it costs. What they walk away with. You name it here so Claude never forgets to point people toward it.

Fourth — your voice. This is the good one. You describe how you talk. Not how you write, how you talk. For me it's: direct, street smart, no corporate softness, short punchy sentences. Think boardroom strategy meets barbershop realness. That's in there.

Fifth — your guardrails. The words you never use. This is as important as anything else in the document. My list has straightforward, it's worth noting, certainly, great question, leverage, dive in, unpack, and about 10 others. You put that list in and Claude stops saying things that make you cringe.

Sixth — your credibility. Your proof. Your wins. The stuff that makes someone trust you.

Seventh — standing instructions. How you want Claude to behave every single time. Always end with a CTA. Always write in my voice. Never use EM dashes. You know the drill."

## SEGMENT 2 — THE CONTENT WORKFLOW (7:18 – 7:30 PM)

**Core idea:** One input. Multiple outputs. Stop starting from scratch every time.

---

"All right. Here's the move. And this is the whole reason I called this session the Content Machine. Because that's what we're building. A machine.

Most people approach content like this. Monday rolls around. They think, what do I post today? They stare at the screen. They write something. Then they need an email. That's another brain mode. Then they need a blog post. That's a third brain mode. Three different kinds of thinking for three pieces of content that all say the same thing.

That's the old way. Here's the new way.

One idea goes in. Multiple pieces come out. That's it. That's the whole system."

---

### The One-to-Many Model

"I call it the one-to-many model. And here's how it works.

You start with a core idea. One topic. Could be something a client asked you this week. Could be a lesson from a recent win. Could be a mistake you made and what you learned. Could be a point of view you've got on something happening in your industry.

One idea. From that one idea — with your Voice Profile loaded — Claude produces:

Three to five social posts. Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram — whatever platforms you're on.
One short email to your list.
A blog post outline. Which also becomes your YouTube script. Your podcast episode. Your LinkedIn article. Pick one.

That's not five pieces of content work. That's one conversation with Claude. You direct it. It drafts. You edit. You post.

Here's the shift that changes everything. You stop being the creator. You become the editor. That's where your time comes back."

---

### The Prompt Stack

"So let me show you what this actually looks like. There's a sequence I run. I call it the prompt stack. Here's how it goes.

Step one — the seed prompt. You give Claude the idea. That's it. One paragraph of context. What's the topic, who's it for, what's the core message.

Step two — social posts. You say: write me five Facebook posts from this. Different hooks, same message. Match my voice.

Step three — email. Turn this into a short email to my list. 150 words max. Direct CTA at the end.

Step four — outline. Give me a five-point blog outline based on this topic.

Four prompts. Same conversation. Same core idea. A week of content.

Now — you don't have to take all of it. You read it, you pick what works, you tweak what doesn't. But you're starting from a draft, not from zero. And that changes everything about how long this takes."

---

### Make it real

"Let me give you the math because I think this lands better with numbers.

Average business owner — if they're doing content at all — 45 minutes to an hour just for one Facebook post. They agonize over it. They write it, delete it, rewrite it. And half the time it still doesn't feel right.

With this system? That same post is one of five options Claude gives you in about 90 seconds. You pick the one that hits, you clean it up, you post it.

A full week of content — five posts, one email, one outline — used to take you most of a Monday morning. With this? Twenty minutes. Maybe less.

The only thing that changes is the setup. And you've got the setup. That's your Voice Profile."

**Chat prompt:** "How long does it actually take you right now to write one Facebook post? Drop a number in the chat. I'm genuinely curious."

*(Read the chat. React to the numbers. "45 minutes? Yeah. That's what I thought. We're fixing that tonight.")*

## SEGMENT 3 — BATCHING IT OUT (7:30 – 7:38 PM)

**Core idea:** Stop creating content daily. Do it once a week. Claude makes this possible.

---

"All right. So now I want to talk about how you actually do this week over week. Because the system only saves you time if you use it consistently. And the secret to using it consistently is batching.

Here's what I mean. Stop creating content every day. That's where people burn out. You're making a decision every single morning — what do I post today? That's not content creation, that's decision fatigue. And it kills people.

Instead — you do one session. Monday morning. Thirty minutes. You come out the other side with a full week of content ready to go. Then you're done. You don't touch it again until next Monday.

Here's what that session looks like."

---

### The Monday Morning Batch

"Step one — you pick three to five topics for the week. Five minutes. You pull these from client questions you answered last week, what's happening in your market, your current offer, something you saw that ticked you off or inspired you. This is all stuff you already know. You're not researching. You're pulling from your own head.

Step two — you run each topic through the prompt stack. One at a time. Social posts, email, outline. You're in and out of each one in about five minutes.

Step three — you do a quick edit pass. Read it, tweak the hook if it needs it, make sure it sounds like you. If your Voice Profile is set up right, this gets shorter every week. You're not rewriting. You're refining.

Step four — you drop it into your scheduler. Scheduled and done.

All in — twenty, twenty-five minutes. Monday morning. Week is covered. You don't think about content again until next week.

And here's the part that I love about this. When you batch it, you stop asking 'what do I post today?' You already handled that. That question is gone. And it frees up brain space for the work that actually moves the needle. All right."
